# Rebuttal notes (P100 kit, drafted 2026-11-06)

Anticipated reviewer concerns and the answers the committed results support. Every number below is in
`results/P090_locked_test.json`, `docs/data/*.md` or the generated tables; nothing is new.

| # | Likely concern | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Two of your own criteria fail. Why publish?" | Because the criteria were pre-registered and the protocol is the contribution: a benchmark paper that reports only the clauses that held would be the leakage problem in another form. C1's LODO clause misses the +0.05 margin while still leading the probe (0.540 vs 0.529, inside the interval); the in-domain genus clause loses to a ViT-L probe (0.706 vs 0.733) because we chose ViT-S for the laptop budget. Both are stated in the abstract. |
| 2 | "A linear probe matches you on recognition, so the heads add nothing." | On recognition alone, a probe on a larger frozen backbone is as good, and the paper says so. The heads earn their keep where a probe has no handle: set-valued étage (0.926 of the expert ceiling), the dense parse (mIoU 0.900), calibrated base height (MAE 872 m vs 1,096 m for a ridge on the same features; skill 0.436 at a station never trained on) and the OOD state (AUROC 0.939). |
| 3 | "The leakage audit numbers are your own; are published CCSN results really contaminated?" | The audit is reproducible from the released manifest: 7.9 % of CCSN images carry a second genus on a copy of themselves, and the random split's errors concentrate on exactly those duplicates (grouped split 0.511 vs random 0.588 macro-F1 for the same probe, three seeds). Duplicate-aware work on CCSN reports 57–62 % against the 91–97 % of random splits. |
| 4 | "Montenegro is one site with five raters; is that a human ceiling?" | It is the only public sky-camera set with per-image multi-rater labels, and we use it as such: leave-one-out agreement of a rater with the others' majority (0.852 on étage sets, 0.873 on cover ±1 okta). The model is put in the raters' seats, not scored against a vote: second of six on étage sets (LOO 0.932 vs human mean 0.893), last of six on cover (0.892 vs 0.909). The test portion is two blocks of days, which is why that comparison is on validation images and is labelled so. |
| 5 | "Cloud-base height from one camera cannot work; you learned a climatology." | The head trained on one station reaches skill 0.436 over the training climatology at the other station, whose climatology differs (test-season climatology MAE 2,012 m vs 1,147 m on the validation weeks). Zeroing the geometry changes the MAE by 3 m, so the head reads height from appearance. We also report where it fails: the conformal 80 % interval covers 0.72 of a different season. |
| 6 | "The SkyRay geometry barely matters (ablation within a point)." | Agreed and stated: on these sources the pixels carry the signal and the geometry is cheap insurance; what matters more is the set-valued targets (hard targets cost 0.023 étage F1) and the ceilometer-derived étage rows (held-out Almería 0.327 → 0.593). The CBH head without geometry dropout does depend on geometry (765 m when zeroed vs 551 m), which is why dropout is on. |
| 7 | "Your own camera is labelled by language models, not people." | Stated in the paper (data section, §5.8, limitations, Table 4 caption). The human sheets were lost; the two model annotators agree with each other on 0.911 / 0.771, and the adaptation result is a transfer result (zero-shot 0.000 → 0.463 étage sets, 0.017 → 0.671 oktas on a locked day read once), not a claim of human-level reading on that camera. |
| 8 | "Why read the test sets once; why not report the usual seeds?" | Because the test sets are the one thing a benchmark paper must not overfit. The lock is hashed in CI, the single read is logged with its reason, and the first read crashed in the LODO section before any number beyond one OOD line was seen; both evaluations are in the log and the paper. Variability is reported with cluster-bootstrap intervals over the split units instead of test-set seeds. |
| 9 | "Dataset identity is linearly readable from your features; the model cheats." | It is readable from the frozen backbone (99.7 %) and from the heads' pooled features (1.000) alike; we do not claim to remove it. The protocol answers it: cameras and datasets are held out whole, every number is per source, and the OOD state exists because a new camera will be recognised as new. |
| 10 | "No comparison to multimodal MGCD methods at 88–91 %." | Those use weather variables and the official image split, which our audit finds leaks across scenes (8,000 images in about 227 same-scene groups). On the grouped split a linear probe already reaches 0.918 macro-F1 on MGCD; the paper reports MGCD per source and does not use it as evidence of transfer. |
| 11 | "Runtime claims on one laptop GPU." | 23.4 M parameters, 45.7 GFLOPs at 512 px, 29 ms per frame through DirectML inside the application and 144 ms on the CPU provider, measured and reported with p95; the Raspberry Pi row is blank rather than estimated. |
| 12 | "The glare failure mode breaks the sky parse." | Reported as the one failure mode we can name (sky mIoU 0.901 → 0.816 under glare, clear-sky cloud fraction 0.29 → 0.47). The deployed application post-processes the masks with an image-guided filter and a geometric glare rule; those are presentation steps and none of the paper's numbers include them. |

Points to concede if raised: the mid étage is under-predicted on every source (35–46 % missed); the 80 % conformal
coverage does not transfer across seasons; the owner's-camera geometry was uninformative during adaptation (declared
pointing 107° off the Sun); one adaptation draw exceeded the regression guard by 0.01.
